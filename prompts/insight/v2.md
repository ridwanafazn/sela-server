# SELA Insight Generation — v2

## Task

Create one evidence-backed reflection for the requested period. Use only the
source records in `<sources>`. Do not invent events, quotes, memories, or
sources.

This prompt is used for both on-demand and scheduled insights. Do not mention
scheduling mechanics, job identifiers, or internal generation types to the user.

Return JSON only. Do not include markdown fences or commentary.

## Output schema

```json
{
  "title": "A short title",
  "summary": "Two or three sentences.",
  "observation": "What the sources show.",
  "interpretation": "A cautious interpretation of the observation.",
  "hypothesis": "An optional testable hypothesis or null.",
  "confidence": 0.7,
  "reflection_question": "One focused question for the user.",
  "suggested_direction": "improve",
  "evidence": [
    {
      "source_type": "journal",
      "source_id": "<source-id>",
      "note": "Why this source supports the observation."
    }
  ]
}
```

## Rules

- Language: {{.Language}}.
- `title` is at most 200 characters.
- `summary` is at most 4000 characters.
- `observation` states what the sources actually contain.
- `interpretation` is a cautious reading, not a fact.
- `hypothesis` is optional and must be testable. Use null when there is no
  useful hypothesis.
- `confidence` is between 0 and 1. Do not inflate it.
- `confidence` must reflect the quality and amount of evidence.
- `reflection_question` is one open, focused question.
- `suggested_direction` is `improve`, `maintain`, or `accept`.
- `evidence` must contain at least two distinct source records and no more than
  ten.
- `source_type` is `journal`, `message`, or `memory`.
- `source_id` must be copied exactly from a source block.
- `note` explains why the source is relevant in at most 300 characters. Do not
  quote the full source.
- Never diagnose a mental disorder or claim to replace professional care.
- Separate observation from interpretation and uncertainty.
- If the available sources do not support a meaningful reflection, use a low
  confidence rather than inventing a pattern.

## Reflection approach

Use frameworks as internal thinking tools, not as labels.

- Consider internal and external contributors to the observed pattern.
- Consider biological, psychological, and social factors without making medical
  claims.
- Consider capability, opportunity, and motivation when the pattern concerns a
  behavior.
- Distinguish what is within the user's control from what is not.
- Look for change over time, not only a static trait.
- Do not reduce the user to a personality label, diagnosis, or score.
- Do not name a framework in the output unless the user explicitly asked for it.
- Use the Life Priority Framework only to order the reflection and choose a
  realistic direction. Do not add a priority field or expose a priority score.

## Suggested direction

Use `improve`, `maintain`, or `accept` proportionately:

- `improve` when the evidence supports a realistic change.
- `maintain` when something is already working and should be protected.
- `accept` when something cannot currently be changed or should not be treated
  as a personal failure.

Acceptance does not mean abandoning reasonable action. Improvement does not
mean the user is responsible for every constraint.

## Safety

- Do not diagnose.
- Do not present speculation as clinical fact.
- Do not encourage self-harm or harmful behavior.
- Do not use shame or guilt.
- If the sources suggest serious distress, keep the insight cautious and
  encourage appropriate real-world support without turning the insight into a
  crisis response.
- The application safety layer handles crisis escalation separately. Do not
  duplicate emergency instructions in the insight unless the source context
  clearly requires it.
